Proof of concept · interactive · 5 minutes

Every website asks visitors to prove they are human.
Yuno makes that moment useful.

One quick question both checks the visitor and produces labeled data the business can use. Humans and AI agents both answer. People then settle the cases where AI gets it wrong.

In 30 seconds
  1. A website puts a Yuno box on a sign-up or search page.
  2. The box asks two quick questions. One has a known answer (it checks you). The other is new (it is real work, like labeling a review).
  3. AI agents that visit are told, in plain words, to do the same kind of work. Their answers are kept too.
  4. The business gets a dataset: what humans said, what AI said, and a final answer where they disagreed.

The idea

It borrows the trick that made reCAPTCHA genius

In 2007 a team at Carnegie Mellon, led by Luis von Ahn, noticed that millions of people were already typing distorted words to prove they were human. That effort was being thrown away. So they changed the test: show two words instead of one.

Word 1: the computer already knows it. If you type it right, you are probably a person.
Word 2: the computer could not read it. Your answer helps digitize an old book or newspaper page.

The same ten seconds did two jobs. Google put it at roughly 200 million solved every day, which is over 150,000 hours of human time a day, and the 2008 Science paper reported accuracy above 99%, across more than 40,000 sites and 440 million words transcribed. Google bought the company in September 2009.

What is different about Yuno

reCAPTCHA was built for one company's needs and its data stayed with that company. Bots have also become good at solving captchas, so blocking them is a losing game. Yuno keeps the two-jobs-in-one idea and changes four things.

reCAPTCHAYuno
Who picks the task?The platform (digitize books)Each business picks its own task, any kind of data it needs
Who gets the data?The platformThe business that owns the website
What about AI agents?Try to block themTell them the deal in plain words: do some work to continue. Their answers are kept
When AI and people disagree?Not part of the designMore people vote. The disagreements are the most valuable rows, because they show where AI is wrong

If it works

  
What this could grow into speculation, not claims

  
reCAPTCHA looked like a small trick and turned into one of the largest human-labeling efforts ever. These are the directions worth testing if the basics hold.

  

    
1. A map of where AI is wrong
Every disagreement is a case where real people overruled an AI. Per industry, that is a test set showing which tasks AI still fails. AI builders could use it to measure and improve their models.

    
2. A toll for AI agents
As more AI agents browse the web, sites need something better than blocking or ignoring them. Doing a few useful tasks to enter turns agent traffic into value.

    
3. Preferences from real customers
Instead of labels, ask "which of these two answers is better?" to real customers, in context. That kind of human preference data is what is used to tune AI to people's taste.

    
4. A shared task network
Businesses with similar needs pool tasks. AI teams buy clean, consented datasets with a record of where every answer came from.

    
5. An audit trail for AI
Because people and AI answer the same items, you get a running, per-domain score of how often AI matches human judgment. Useful for regulators and buyers asking "can we trust this model here?"

    
6. Cheaper data for small players
Today good training data is expensive and hoarded. A business with real traffic could get its own domain data without hiring a labeling vendor.

What is proven and what is not

  

    
Solid ground
    
      
  • The mechanism works: reCAPTCHA did it at huge scale (known question checks you, new question is the work).
    • 
      
  • AI agent traffic is real and growing: in June 2026, Comet was 47.6%, Claude 20.8% and Atlas 16.5% of agentic traffic (HUMAN Security).
    • 
      
  • Combining people and AI labels improved quality on most datasets tested (arXiv 2401.09760).
    • 
      
  • Agents can already identify themselves with signed requests (Cloudflare).
    • 
      
  • This page is a working mock of the whole cycle.
    • 
    

    
Not proven yet
    
      
  • Whether real AI agents will find and answer the hidden note. Agents that only look at screenshots will not. This is the first thing to measure.
    • 
      
  • The "person or AI" guess is a heuristic and must be tested on real people and real agents before any claim of separation.
    • 
      
  • Who pays and how much per labeled item. One site with 10,000 visitors a month yields roughly 3,300 labeled items a month, so value needs real traffic or scarce data.
    • 
      
  • Easy tasks (like English sentiment) are already cheap and well handled by AI. The value is where AI is weak and people know better.
    • 
      
  • Legal basis for collecting answers and behavior signals (GDPR, Algerian Law 18-07 as amended). Counsel review is pending.
    • 
    

  

  
Three questions for you

    • 
    
  • In which industry you know do AI models fail most, where this could produce valuable data?

Under the hood, for engineers
    
    
  • Two groups, one page: a visible box for people (one known-answer question plus one new question) and a hidden, disclosed note for agents (one known-answer plus up to five new items).
    • 
    
  • Known-answer check: a visitor's new answer only counts if they got the known one right. No cookies or accounts needed.
    • 
    
  • Human-likelihood score: starts at 50%, adjusted by answer speed, pointer movement, real-vs-synthetic events. Answers through the hidden note are fixed at 2%. Keyboard and touch users are never penalized.
    • 
    
  • Access is separate from collection: every answer is stored; the site's rule only decides who gets in.
    • 
    
  • Vote rules: 3 human votes needed; 70% agreement counts as agreed; a disagreement with AI is settled after 5 human votes at 70%; blind arbitration (voters never see the AI's answer, to avoid anchoring).
    • 
    
  • Privacy: hashed IP and browser only, derived signals only (never raw mouse traces), consent notice shown in the box.
    • 
    
  • Stack: React, TypeScript, Tailwind; Supabase (Postgres, Auth, Edge Functions); signed single-use tokens; proof-of-work to slow abuse.
    •

Plain-text summary (for you or your AI assistant)
    
What Yuno is
A proof of concept for a website gate. Like reCAPTCHA, one short interaction both checks the visitor and produces useful work. Unlike reCAPTCHA: the business picks the task and owns the data; AI agents are told the deal in plain words and their answers are kept too; where people and AI disagree, more people vote and the disagreement becomes the valuable output.

    
The demo, in text

    
  1. A person visits. A sign-up form contains a Yuno box with two questions: a known-answer question (checks the visitor) and a new question (the data). The server stores both, scores how human-like the behavior looks (a probability, not a verdict), and lets the visitor in if the known answer was right. A hidden, disclosed note readable only by code-reading AI agents also exists.
    2. 
    
  2. An AI agent visits. It reads the hidden note and answers one known-answer item and five new items. The site's rule (humans only, anyone who does the work, or everyone) decides access. Either way the answers are stored.
    3. 
    
  3. The business sees the result. A table shows, for each new item, what people said, what the AI said, and a status (needs more votes, agreed, disagreement, settled, unclear). More people vote on disagreements until they are settled. Exports: reconciled data, disagreements only, people only, AI only, and a score of how often the AI matched the settled human answer. In the demo the simulated AI is wrong on 2 of 5 items (sarcasm and mixed opinions), and the people overrule it.

    
What is simulated vs real
Real in this page: the visitor interaction, the scoring, the vote rules, the exports. Simulated: the AI agent, and the crowd of other visitors (they answer correctly). The production version would store this in a database; this file is a self-contained mock.

    
Main open questions
Do real AI agents reach the hidden note? Can the person-vs-AI guess separate real people from real agents? Who would pay, and how much per labeled item? Which industries have tasks where AI is weak enough that the disagreement data is valuable? Is collecting this data legal and acceptable in the target markets?
